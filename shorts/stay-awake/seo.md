## Description
How long can you stay awake? In 1964, a student named Randy Gardner went 264 hours without sleep.
But your brain can switch off for a few seconds while your eyes stay open. That's called a microsleep.

Three cartoon contestants try to win $100,000 by being the last one awake. By day two, the cameras catch all three dozing off with their eyes wide open.

In this Short:
- what a microsleep is, and why you might not notice one
- the 1964 experiment: 264 hours awake, 11 days
- why Guinness stopped tracking sleep-deprivation records in 1996

The challenge and the characters are made up. The facts are real:
Randy Gardner's 264 hours (NPR): https://www.npr.org/2024/01/28/1227217274/sleep-deprivation-record
Microsleep (Sleep Foundation): https://www.sleepfoundation.org/how-sleep-works/microsleep

Please don't try a stay-awake challenge. Going without sleep is harmful.
